Blood Bank follows a clear operational flow so staff can manage donor data, blood units, screening, storage, and blood movement in a structured way. The system is designed to keep each step connected so users can easily understand where a blood unit came from, what happened to it, and where it is currently stored.

### 1. Donor or Source Identification

The workflow begins when a blood unit is created from an internal donor collection or received from an external blood bank.

- Internal collection is linked to a donor record.
- External blood units are linked to the source blood bank and external reference details.
- The system captures the basic information needed to track the unit correctly from the beginning.

This step ensures that every blood unit has a clear origin and can be traced later when needed.

### 2. Blood Unit Registration

Once the source is selected, the blood unit is added into the system.

- The unit is registered with blood group, component type, volume, and collection or received date.
- Storage details are recorded so the unit can be placed in the correct physical location.
- A unique unit reference is generated to identify the blood bag in the system.

This helps the team keep blood units organized and avoid manual tracking.

### 3. QR Assignment and Tracking

Each blood unit can be linked to a QR code for quick identification.

- The QR code is attached during blood unit creation.
- Staff can scan the QR code to identify the unit faster during daily operations.
- QR-based tracking helps reduce lookup time and improves accuracy when handling blood units.

This makes it easier to move between inventory, lab testing, and storage operations without confusion.

### 4. Screening and Testing

After a blood unit is registered, it moves into the screening workflow.

- The unit is marked as pending screening at the beginning.
- Lab staff record screening results for the mandatory tests.
- The unit status changes based on the test outcome.

Typical screening outcomes include:
- pending tests
- screening in progress
- screening safe
- quality check failure or other problem states

This step is important because only safe and approved units should continue into active inventory.

### 5. Storage Placement

After screening, the blood unit is stored in the correct equipment or storage location.

- The system records the building, zone, and equipment where the unit is kept.
- If a unit is moved later, the storage movement is tracked separately.
- Staff can always see where the unit is currently stored.

This keeps physical storage and digital tracking aligned.

### 6. Inventory Monitoring

The inventory view helps staff monitor available blood units across different blood groups.

- The dashboard shows stock summary and unit counts.
- Blood group cards provide a quick view of availability.
- Staff can open a blood group to see all related units in a list.

This allows the team to quickly understand stock position and act when a blood group is low or unavailable.

### 7. Reservation, Issue, and Return

When a blood unit is needed for a patient, it can move through reservation and issue workflows.

- A unit can be reserved for a specific request.
- A reserved unit can then be issued when required.
- If a unit is returned unused, it can be brought back into the workflow.
- Returned units can be marked for re-testing before reuse when needed.

This workflow helps the team manage actual hospital usage without losing track of the unit’s status.

### 8. Discard and Expiry Handling

Some units may not remain usable because of expiry, failed screening, damage, or other quality issues.

- The system supports discard handling with proper reason tracking.
- Expired or unusable units are kept separate from active inventory.
- Discarded units remain visible in the disposed view for audit and review.

This ensures unsafe or unusable units do not stay in the active stock list.

### 9. Movement and Audit History

Every important change is tracked in the system.

- Collection history is recorded.
- Screening history is recorded.
- Storage movement history is recorded separately.
- Issue, return, and discard actions are also tracked.

This gives staff a complete view of what happened to the unit over time.

### 10. Communication and Follow-Up

The system also supports communication-related follow-up for donors.

- Phone call history can be recorded.
- WhatsApp or other donor communication can be tracked.
- Staff can note donor responses, follow-up reminders, and remarks.

This helps the team maintain better donor engagement and future follow-up.

### 11. Settings and Master Data

Supporting values used throughout the system are managed through settings and master data.

- Collecting locations
- Storage locations
- Blood component types
- Discard reasons
- Other configurable system values

These master records help keep the workflow consistent and easy to manage.

### 12. Daily Operation Flow

In practice, the Blood Bank workflow usually follows this order:

- register or identify the source
- create the blood unit
- assign QR tracking
- screen and test the unit
- store the unit in the right location
- reserve or issue when required
- handle return, discard, or expiry when needed

This end-to-end flow helps staff manage blood bank operations in a clear and dependable way.
